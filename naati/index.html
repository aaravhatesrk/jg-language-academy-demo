About the NAATI CCL Exam

The Canadian English Language The National Accreditation Authority for Translators and Interpreters (NAATI) Credentialed Community Language (CCL) test assesses your ability to interpret and translate conversations in real-life community settings. Passing this test grants 5 PR points under Australia’s migration points system.

Community Interpretation

Focuses on real-life conversations in areas like immigration, healthcare, education, legal services, and government settings.

Cultural Competency

Emphasizes understanding cultural nuances, delivering accurate and contextually appropriate translations for diverse communities.

NAATI CCL Test Format

The NAATI CCL test consists of two recorded dialogues, each approximately 300 words long, between an English speaker and a non-English speaker. Candidates must interpret and translate in both directions

  • Total Duration: 20 minutes.
  • Scoring: Total 90 marks, with a minimum of 29 in each dialogue and an overall score of 63 or more required to pass

Tips for NAATI CCL Success

  • Listening
  • Reading
  • Writing
  • Speaking

listening Tips

  • Listen actively and focus on key details.
  • Understand cultural and contextual meanings.
  • Take structured notes while listening.
  • Practice with real-life community dialogues.

reading Tips


  • Skim the passage first to get the main idea.
  • Focus on keywords in the questions to find answers faster.
  • Understand context clues for unknown words.
  • Read the answer choices carefully to avoid traps.

writing Tips

  • Task 1 (Email Writing): Follow the format (Greeting, Purpose, Details, Closing).
  • Task 2 (Survey Response): Give clear reasons and examples for your choice.
  • Use varied sentence structures and correct grammar.
  • Plan before writing; check for spelling and coherence.

speaking Tips

Format Overview

  • Speak clearly and at a natural pace.
  • Organize answers logically (Introduction, Main Points, Conclusion).
  • Use a mix of vocabulary and sentence structures.
  • If unsure, paraphrase or explain in another way instead of staying silent.
